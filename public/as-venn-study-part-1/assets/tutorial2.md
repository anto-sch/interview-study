#### Visual Cues

All tasks include visual cues to help you find the solution. Each cue offers support in two ways, text highlighting and logical diagrams.

##### First Cue: Highlighting

The first type of cue is the color coding of the task text. The "if" and "then" parts of the premises and the conclusion are highlighted in color. If two parts are highlighted in the same color, they are either the same statements or a statement and its negation. In case an if-part or then-part is negated, the corresponding text is <ins>underlined</ins>. An example:

"To be <mark style="background-color: #874fff88">allowed to buy alcohol in Germany</mark>, you must <mark style="background-color: #24CB7188">be of legal age</mark>. Anyone <ins><mark style="background-color: #FF723788">under the age of 18</mark></ins> is considered a <ins><mark style="background-color: #24CB7188">minor</mark></ins>. Anyone who is <mark style="background-color: #874fff88">allowed to buy alcohol in Germany</mark> must be <mark style="background-color: #FF723788">18 years of age or older</mark>."

*Why is this important?* This cue is intended to help you reliably identify the if-then form of conditional sentences, even in cases where negations are present. 

##### Second Cue: Venn Diagrams of Conditionals

The second type of cue consists of set diagrams, which are displayed next to the text in addition to the color coding. Each premise and conclusion has its own set diagram. It shows the "if" part of the conditional as a set that is contained in the set of the "then" part by representing the conditional as two intersecting sets and shading (hatching) the area of the "if" part. These diagrams are also called Venn diagrams. 

An example: The following Venn diagram is displayed for the conditional "To be <mark style="background-color: #874fff88">allowed to buy alcohol in Germany</mark>, you must <mark style="background-color: #24CB7188">be of legal age</mark>":

If an area is shaded, it means that the situations contained within that subset cannot exist. In the example, the shading makes it clear that there can be no one who is allowed to buy alcohol but is not of legal age. It is also said that legal age is a necessary condition for purchasing alcohol. There may be further restrictions. For example, you must not be legally incapacitated. Conversely, being allowed to purchase alcohol is a sufficient condition for inferring legal age.

*Why is this important?* This cue is intended to help avoiding confusing sufficient and necessary conditions.
